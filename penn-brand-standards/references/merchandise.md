# Penn-Branded Merchandise and Promotional Products

Source: *On Your Marks: Penn Branding & Licensing Toolkit for Schools, Centers & Departments* (Penn Business Services, August 2026), reviewed by Penn Licensing. Questions: Penn Licensing, PennBranding@upenn.edu.

Read this whenever a task involves a physical or promotional item that carries a Penn logo, wordmark, or name: apparel, giveaways, event swag, mockups of any of these, or advice on ordering them. Any such item counts as Penn-branded merchandise, whether it's sold ("retail") or given away by a school, center, or department ("promotional"). These rules add to the brand standards in `SKILL.md`; they don't replace them.

**Student organizations** follow the student brand guidelines from the Office of Student Affairs, which include the student version of this toolkit: https://universitylife.upenn.edu/student-brand-guidelines/. Point student groups there instead of applying this file.

## Your role as the agent

A mockup you produce is a concept for discussion, never production artwork. Never tell the user a design is "approved" or "compliant." Say it follows the toolkit's rules and still needs Penn Licensing approval. Point the user to the right next step: official logo files, a licensed vendor, and Penn Licensing for questions. In a mockup, show the logo as a clearly labeled placeholder (for example, "Official [center] logo here") unless the user supplies the official file; never draw, trace, or retype it.

## The three requirements

Every promotional product bearing Penn marks must:

1. **Comply with the Penn Brand Guidelines** (the rest of this skill).
2. **Be purchased from a licensed Penn promotional vendor.** Ordering from an unlicensed vendor puts the school, center, or department out of University compliance.
3. **Be approved through Penn's Trademark Licensing Program.** The licensed vendor submits the final artwork to Penn Licensing; the user doesn't submit anything. Penn Licensing, part of the Division of Business Services, has final approval of all Penn-branded merchandise designs. Approval from the user's own team or communications office does not replace it.

## Design rules

- **Official logos only, unaltered.** Use the approved University, school, center, or department logo files. Download them from https://branding.web-resources.upenn.edu/logos-and-branding/download-penn-logos or get them from the school's or center's communications office. Schools, centers, and departments may not create custom logos. Wharton and Penn Medicine have additional guidelines for department and program name placement; send those users to their communications office.
- **Formal configuration by default.** Shield to the left of the wordmark. Use the stacked lockup (shield above wordmark) only when the print area is too small for the formal one.
- **Full shield, never simplified.** The simplified shield (without the book, dolphin, and circle details) should not be used on branded merchandise. Don't propose it. If a vendor says the full shield can't be rendered, for example in embroidery, the user should ask Penn Licensing to recommend a vendor that can. Simplified *school* logos are fine on merchandise, because they keep the full formal shield.
- **Official logo dominant.** The full University, school, center, or department logo (shield and wordmark together) is the main visual. This doesn't permit using the "Penn" wordmark on its own.
- **Keep other artwork separate.** Extra artwork is only for a specific program or event and must include its name and date or year. Place it physically apart from the Penn mark, for example the Penn logo on the front and the event graphic on the back. The same applies to co-branded partner logos and apparel brand logos (such as a Nike swoosh): use opposite sides of the chest, a sleeve, or the back.
- **Never integrate or take apart Penn marks.** Don't use a Penn mark as part of a larger composition, use the shield as a letter or number, place it inside a QR code, or use the book, dolphin, or chevron as standalone graphic elements.
- **Colors.** Penn Red and Penn Blue are encouraged (values and print specs in `SKILL.md`).
- **Type.** A Penn wordmark combines two typefaces: the department or division name is set in Perpetua, and "University of Pennsylvania" in ITC Stone Serif. Where a vendor or designer has to specify a font, specify Perpetua (or a close serif) for the department or division name. Never use script, italic, or novelty fonts.

## Not allowed on merchandise

- **The Split P.** It's an Athletics mark and isn't available to schools, centers, or departments. Use their own logo or the Penn shield and wordmark instead.
- **The LOVE statue.** Penn doesn't own its trademark and can't approve designs that show it (per the Office of General Counsel). The Button, Ben on the Bench, and other Penn landmarks may be used.
- **Taglines and quotes.** Only quotes from Benjamin Franklin or a program's namesake are allowed. Other than an event or anniversary name and date (for example, "10th Anniversary Gala 2026"), don't add taglines or promotional wording. A slogan such as "A Decade of Impact" counts as a tagline.

## Ordering and timeline

- **Choose a licensed vendor** from Penn's list of licensed promotional vendors: https://www.business-services.upenn.edu/services/penn-branded-merchandise-licensing/information-university-departments-and-student-groups. If the item the user needs isn't available from one, they should contact Penn Licensing to discuss options rather than going elsewhere. Vendors who want to become licensed can apply at https://www.business-services.upenn.edu/services/how-become-penn-licensee.
- **No blank apparel.** Users can't supply their own blank items for a vendor to decorate. Trademark-bearing merchandise must be ordered directly from a licensed vendor.
- **Apparel brands.** Not every consumer apparel brand holds a Penn license, and not every licensed vendor carries every approved brand. For a specific brand, the user should ask Penn Licensing about its status.
- **Plan the schedule backward from the in-hands date.** Penn Licensing review takes at least two business days from a complete, compliant submission. If the submission has an incorrect logo file or unapproved design elements, it's returned and the clock restarts when the vendor resubmits. Review time doesn't include production or shipping, so the user should confirm the full timeline with the vendor and build in extra time, especially for a first order or a complex design.
- **Send production-ready artwork** within the vendor's turnaround window.
- **Unsure? Ask first.** If the user isn't sure a design complies, suggest they email PennBranding@upenn.edu for preapproval before sending artwork to the vendor. For a deeper dive, Penn Licensing holds monthly virtual Drop-In sessions (sign up at https://forms.cloud.microsoft/r/7BZMWa20rn).

## Exceptions

Penn Licensing evaluates merchandise exceptions individually (for example, an anniversary logo or a unique event) and rarely approves them. Never assume or suggest that one will apply. If the user believes their design warrants one, tell them to raise it early with PennBranding@upenn.edu and not to plan the project around it being granted.

## Merchandise checklist

- [ ] Official, unaltered logo file in the formal configuration (stacked only for small print areas)
- [ ] Full shield, not the simplified shield
- [ ] Official logo is the dominant visual; any event artwork includes the event name and date and is physically separate
- [ ] No Split P, LOVE statue, or taglines beyond a Franklin or namesake quote
- [ ] Ordered from a licensed vendor, which submits the artwork to Penn Licensing
- [ ] Schedule includes at least two business days for Penn Licensing review, plus production and shipping
